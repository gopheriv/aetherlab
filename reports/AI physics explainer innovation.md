# Win on verified physics, not tutoring chat

Nearly every piece of AetherLab now has a free or cheap counterpart somewhere. Socratic tutoring is free from OpenAI, Google and Anthropic. ChatGPT gives away slider-driven visuals for 70+ concepts. NotebookLM makes narrated animated explainers. At least a dozen open-source and paid tools turn prompts into Manim code. What no product found in this research combines is the part hardest to copy: **physics you can check**. That means a numeric ground truth driving the motion, a verifier, textbook citations and an interactive simulation, all from one run and able to work offline. The research gives three reasons this is where to compete. Frontier LLMs still get only about 30–57% of multi-step physics answers right. Pixel-video generators score about 30% on physical plausibility. And polished explainer videos leave learners just as confident when the content is wrong, so an unverified error in a smooth animation does more harm than the same error in text. The published agent literature has settled on the architecture AetherLab already uses (planner, coder, render-repair, vision critic). Its ablations also show where the remaining quality points are: spatial anchoring for the critic, scoped repair, targeted API retrieval, and memory of past fixes. Learning science says the output must interrupt itself with prediction, retrieval and self-explanation, and must not end as a passive video. The roadmap below follows that order. First make correctness measurable and visible. Then make every lesson active. Only then widen reach through offline packaging, teacher tooling and a structured scene format that can drive both Manim and live simulation.

## Free frontier features now cover the tutor, the slider and the narrated video

The biggest competitive shift of 2025–26 is that the general-purpose labs gave away most of the obvious features. **ChatGPT Study Mode** (July 2025) withholds answers in favor of Socratic hints on free and paid plans ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/29/openai-launches-study-mode-in-chatgpt)). **Gemini Guided Learning** runs on the pedagogy-tuned LearnLM model and is free for all Gemini users ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/)). Claude's Learning Mode ships free at partner universities ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education?)). In March 2026 OpenAI added **interactive visuals for 70+ math and science concepts**, such as Ohm's law and exponential decay, where users move variables and watch formulas update. It is free to every logged-in user, but it is a fixed set of modules, not arbitrary generated simulations ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-now-explains-math-and-physics-with-interactive-visualizations/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/chatgpt-will-now-generate-interactive-visuals-to-help-you-with-math-and-science-concepts-170000520.html)). Google's **NotebookLM Cinematic Video Overviews** (March 2026) produce fully animated, narrated explainers from uploaded PDFs using Gemini 3 and Veo 3. They launched only for AI Ultra subscribers, and reviewers call them "early-competent" with "occasional visual inaccuracies", not recommended where accuracy is paramount ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/notebooklm-launches-cinematic-video-overviews/); [buildfastwithai](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/notebooklm-cinematic-video-overview-full-guide-2026)).

The economic effect is already visible. Chegg's full-year 2025 revenue fell **39% to $376.9M** and headcount fell from 3,191 to 595 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/27/chegg-slashes-45percent-of-workforce-blames-new-realities-of-ai.html); [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/chegg-layoffs-strategic-alternatives-google-ai/804192/)). Q2 2026 revenue was down another **51% year on year**, and the company is pivoting to employability ([Chegg Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495426000085/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm)). Selling answers is finished as a business. So is selling Socratic chat, because the labs now give it away.

Prompt-to-Manim generation is crowded too. **Math-To-Manim** (MIT, about 2.6k GitHub stars) is AetherLab's closest conceptual rival. It runs reverse prerequisite mapping, then curriculum, then math validation, then camera planning, then code and render. It has four interchangeable LLM backends and exposes CLI, MCP and REST interfaces ([GitHub](https://github.com/HarleyCoops/Math-To-Manim)). **Animo** is the closest *product* analogue. It is a small desktop app with local rendering, physics templates, voiceover via manim-voiceover, and a **$49 one-time** price, though it still relies on a cloud AI subscription or credits ([animo.video](https://animo.video/)). Its open-source parent, Generative Manim, is training open models with Manim rendering as the reward signal ([GitHub](https://github.com/marcelo-earth/generative-manim)). AnimG sells browser-based freemium rendering ([animg.app](https://animg.app/en)).

The two funded YC startups have left rigorous STEM. **Knowlify** claims 223K+ videos but now targets business training ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OAm-knowlify-the-elevenlabs-for-explainer-videos)). **Lamina Labs/Simi** abandoned Manim for its own whiteboard renderer because its CTO said Manim "was built before the LLM era" and is hard to fine-tune for ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/REC-simi-the-fastest-ai-whiteboard-explainer-video-generator); [mer.vin](https://mer.vin/2026/05/simi-by-lamina-labs-whiteboard-explainer-videos-from-prompts-and-documents/)).

The simulation incumbents have reach but rigid catalogs. **PhET** delivers **250M+ simulation runs a year** from about 170 hand-built sims and has just launched a premium "PhET Studio" customization product ([PhET Impact Report 2025](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Impact_Report_2025.pdf)). Brilliant charges $27.99/month or $161.88/year and bolts its Koji AI tutor onto fixed courses ([Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/help/pricing-and-plans/how-much-does-brilliant-premium-cost/)). Wolfram's Notebook Assistant pairs LLMs with "computation-augmented generation", which is the closest analogue to AetherLab's numeric ground truth, but at professional price points ([Wolfram](https://www.wolfram.com/notebook-assistant-llm-kit/)).

| Capability | ChatGPT / Gemini / NotebookLM | Math-To-Manim / Animo / AnimG | PhET / Brilliant | AetherLab |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Socratic tutor chat | Free, strong | None | Koji (Brilliant) | Present, but no longer differentiating |
| Narrated animated lesson | NotebookLM Cinematic (Ultra, "visual inaccuracies") | Yes, unverified code | No | Yes, multi-chapter |
| Interactive sliders | ChatGPT: ~70 fixed concepts | Animo: 3D object viewing only | ~170 hand-built sims | Any problem, generated |
| Numeric/physics verification | None exposed | Math validation (Math-To-Manim) | Hand-validated | Numeric ground truth + verifier |
| Curriculum citations | User uploads only | None | Own courses | OpenStax sections by default |
| Offline / local model | No | Math-To-Manim Grok "offline mode" only | PhET HTML5 works offline | Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint |

The empty cells show where to compete. AetherLab should stop trying to win on tutoring dialogue or basic Ohm's-law widgets. It should compete on the **long tail of arbitrary problems**, on **verification the user can see**, and on **offline and privacy-first operation**. None of the funded players occupies that ground. The risks are real. The labs ship free STEM features every few months. Math-To-Manim is free and improving. A Windows-only desktop app misses Chromebook-heavy K-12 classrooms.

## Published agents converge on AetherLab's pipeline, and their ablations show where quality is lost

Academic work on LLM-generated educational video has settled on the shape AetherLab already uses. In **TheoremExplainAgent** (ACL 2025), a planner writes a multi-scene storyboard and narration, and a coder writes Manim with up to five error-correction retries. With o3-mini it reached a **93.8% render success rate** on 240 STEM theorems. GPT-4o managed 55%, and Claude 3.5 Sonnet only 2.1%. The failure taxonomy has two dominant classes: **hallucinated Manim APIs**, then **LaTeX errors**. Most successful videos still had layout problems ([arXiv 2502.19400](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19400v1)). Two findings stand out. Generic Manim-documentation RAG **lowered** GPT-4o's success from 55% to 45.8%, because irrelevant retrievals inflated the context. Visualizing an explanation also "exposes deeper reasoning flaws that text-based explanations fail to reveal" ([arXiv 2502.19400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19400)).

**Code2Video** (Show Lab) offers the most useful ablations for AetherLab's backlog. It adds a VLM critic that places elements on a **6×6 grid of visual anchor points** (a 4×4 grid underperformed and 8×8 caused clutter). It also adds "ScopeRefine", a repair loop that escalates from the failing line to its block to a full section regeneration ([arXiv 2510.01174](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174)).

| Configuration (Code2Video) | Aesthetics | TeachQuiz |
|---|---|---|
| Human 3Blue1Brown videos | 99.7 | 97.1 |
| Veo3 pixel-space video | 9.0 | 2.5 |
| Direct GPT-5 code generation | 32.7 | 36.5 |
| Full agent, Claude Opus | 87.9 | 86.0 |
| Removing the planner | −40.9 | −41.5 |
| Removing the external asset database | −10.9 | −30.0 |
| Removing visual anchors | −9.8 | −26.8 |
| Removing the critic | −6.5 | −21.3 |

Source: [arXiv 2510.01174](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174). Removing ScopeRefine made generation **2.5–5.6× slower** and raised token use 1.4–1.7×.

Mapped onto AetherLab, each row is a feature. The planner is AetherLab's planner-first pipeline. The asset database corresponds to AetherLab's curated numeric-driven component library (pendulum, orbit, double slit, lens and others). That row is the strongest evidence in the literature for **expanding the component library and preferring it over free-form code**. Visual anchors are the gap: AetherLab's vision critic judges frames without a coordinate grid.

**ManimAgent** (2026) adds a dual-channel memory: distilled rationales from scenes that succeeded, plus validated "known pitfalls" from crash-to-fix histories. It **cut repair rounds from 12.2 to 6.5** and raised first-pass human acceptance to 84.9% ([arXiv 2606.30296](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30296)). Its critic loops are bounded at two text and two visual rounds, with auto-pass at a score of 90, which argues against open-ended self-healing.

A 2026 Nottingham Trent study showed that an **open Qwen3-Coder-30B**, with GRPO training plus renderer-in-the-loop repair that retrieves documentation for *the specific functions in the failing code*, reached **94% render success**. That beat a GPT-4.1 baseline. Inference-time loops gained more than fine-tuning alone ([arXiv 2604.18364](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18364)). The **ManiBench** benchmark found the median model executes only about a third of the time, and Qwen-2.5-Coder scored 0%. A prompt listing forbidden ManimGL constructs (`ShowCreation` instead of `Create`, for example) "eliminates version conflicts entirely" ([arXiv 2603.13251](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13251)). **PhysicsSolutionAgent**, the only physics-specific Manim agent found, reports that its VLM critic itself made "errors in how visual content is interpreted" ([arXiv 2601.13453](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2601.13453)).

Evaluation research warns against trusting a VLM as the judge of correctness. On TheoremExplainBench, automatic scores correlated with human raters at **ρ = 0.72 for visual relevance** but only **0.42 for layout** and **0.14 for accuracy and depth**. Human-made videos scored the same 0.77 as the agents, so the metric saturates ([arXiv 2502.19400](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19400v1)). Code2Video's **TeachQuiz** is more robust. It first makes a VLM "unlearn" a concept, then has it watch the video and answer 10 MCQs, and scores the gain ([arXiv 2510.01174](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174)). Correctness therefore has to come from deterministic checks, not from judges.

The physics-reasoning benchmarks show why AetherLab's verifier is essential. On **PhysReason** the best models reach 53–57% answer-level accuracy overall and about **30–32% on hard problems** (15.6 steps on average). The most frequent error classes are **calculation errors and misapplied theorems** ([arXiv 2502.12054](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12054v1)). The best overall score on **UGPhysics** is 49.8% ([arXiv 2502.00334](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00334v2)). Multi-agent review adds only about 2–3 points ([arXiv 2508.00079](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00079)). The emerging answer is tool-grounded checking: **Pint for units and SymPy for symbolic steps** ([PRiSM, arXiv 2512.05930](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05930)), and per-step SymPy scripts as a "weak verifier" ([TPBench, arXiv 2506.20729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20729)).

On motion, pixel-space video fails badly. The best model scores **32.6% joint** on VideoPhy-2 ([arXiv 2503.06800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06800)) and **29.5%** on Physics-IQ ([arXiv 2501.09038](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09038)). Even when models write simulator code, they "struggle to accurately infer physical parameters" ([VisPhyWorld, arXiv 2602.13294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13294)). The NEWTON authors put it well: "text prompts are lossy compression of the physical world" ([arXiv 2605.18396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18396)). This supports AetherLab's design choice to let the LLM choose parameters while a solver determines the motion.

This research also reveals an open gap: **no benchmark measures the physical correctness of code-rendered educational animations**. Physics is just one category inside TheoremExplainBench and Code2Video's MMMC benchmark. AetherLab could define and publish that benchmark.

## Polished video creates false confidence, so each lesson must interrupt itself

The learning-science evidence cuts both for and against AetherLab's video-first output. Well-designed media does help. Mayer's lab studies report median effects of **d = 1.30 for temporal contiguity** (highlight a thing when narration names it), 0.87 for redundancy (don't print the narration on screen), 0.70 for segmenting, 0.72 for modality and 0.79 for a conversational voice ([Mayer 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jcal.12197)). Animations beat static pictures by **d = 0.37** on average, and by more when they show the actual process rather than decorate it ([Höffler & Leutner 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475207001077)). These are mostly short lab studies with immediate tests, so classroom effects will be smaller. No study has tested *AI-generated* narrated animations against these principles.

The risk is fluency. Kulgemeyer and Wittwer showed 149 physics learners either a force video containing misconceptions or a correct one matched for clarity and length. Both groups reported **the same perceived understanding**, but the misconception group left with more misconceptions. The authors concluded explainer videos should not be standalone learning tools ([Int. J. Sci. Math. Educ.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8932681/)). In Harvard physics, students in active classes learned more but *felt* they learned less than students in polished lectures ([Deslauriers et al., PNAS 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.1821936116)). For AetherLab, an LLM physics error inside a beautiful Manim video is worse than the same error in text, because the polish hides it. In-app satisfaction ratings are also the wrong success metric.

Physics education research shows what to build instead. Interactive-engagement courses roughly **doubled normalized FCI gains (0.48 vs 0.23)** across 6,542 students ([Hake 1998](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED441679)). Active learning raises STEM exam performance by **0.47 SD** ([Freeman et al., PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1319030111)). Muller's randomized trial with 364 physics students found that videos which **state and refute the common misconception** beat plain exposition by an effect size of **0.79**, and a student–tutor **dialogue** format by **0.83**. Novices gained most, and experts were not harmed ([Muller et al. 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2007.00248.x)). Several cheap-to-implement techniques have meta-analytic support. Self-explanation prompts yield **g = 0.55** ([Bisra et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x)). Worked examples yield **g = 0.48** and should be faded as expertise grows ([Barbieri et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1)). Interleaving easily confused problem types yields **g = 0.42** ([Brunmair & Richter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/)). Trying a problem before instruction (productive failure) yields **d = 0.36** on conceptual transfer ([Sinha & Kapur](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105)). Practice testing consistently beats restudying ([Adesope et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654316689306)).

Simulations earn their place when they have goals. Replacing real circuit equipment with PhET produced equal or better conceptual learning ([SERC](https://serc.carleton.edu/sp/library/phet/examples/example2.html)). PhET's design research says to set specific goals without over-constraining exploration ([Wieman et al., Science](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1161948)).

The AI-tutoring evidence splits sharply by design, and the best result is almost a blueprint for AetherLab. In a Harvard intro-physics crossover RCT (N = 194), an AI tutor at home beat an in-class *active-learning* lesson by **0.73–1.3 SD** ([Kestin et al., Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)). The details of how it worked matter most. The authors found "a system prompt could not reliably provide enough structure", so **the platform enforced step-by-step scaffolding**. To prevent hallucination they injected **instructor-written step-by-step solutions** rather than letting GPT-4 solve ([preprint](https://projects.panickssery.com/docs/kestin-2024-ai_tutoring_harvard_physics.pdf)). AetherLab's numeric ground truth and verifier can fill the role of those pre-written solutions. The study was single-site and two weeks long, with no pre-registration.

The counter-evidence is just as specific. An unguarded ChatGPT-like tutor raised practice scores 48% but **lowered unassisted exam scores by 17%**, while a hints-only tutor largely avoided the harm ([Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). A two-year cluster RCT of Khanmigo found only **0.02–0.08 SD**. There, 39.4% of student messages were bare answers and only 14.5% involved reasoning ([NBER w35620 via WINS](https://www.winssolutions.org/khanmigo-ai-tutoring-two-year-trial/)). LLMs used as a substitute rather than a complement **widened gaps** between low- and high-prior-knowledge students ([Lehmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047)). A widely shared meta-analysis claiming g = 0.867 for ChatGPT has been **retracted** ([Nature HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04787-y)). The lesson for AetherLab is to design in forced reasoning moments rather than rely on an open chat box. Its efficacy claims should be modest: "built on the Kestin/Bastani principles", not "doubles learning".

## The revenue is in exam prep and out-of-field physics teaching

Market-research estimates for "AI in education" disagree so much they mean little. 2025 figures range from **$2.2B to $18.9B** depending on the firm ([MarketsandMarkets](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-education-market-surges-5-140000060.html); [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/education-ai)). The real spending sits in **private tutoring, about $131–134B in 2025** ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/private-tutoring-market); [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/academic-tutoring)), and in exam prep. Edtech venture funding was only **$2.6B in 2025**, and Q1 2026 was a "slow start" at $512M ([HolonIQ](https://www.holoniq.com/notes/512m-in-q1-signals-a-slow-start-to-2026-with-capital-continuing-to-favor-ai-enabled-career-aligned-platforms)). That favors a low-burn product whose marginal cost is near zero in Ollama mode.

Three beachheads stand out. **AP Physics 1** had 174,992 test-takers in 2025 ([College Board](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-physics-1-score-distributions.pdf)). Its 2026 pass rate of 68% is the lowest of the physics exams, and its **17% share of 1s** is the highest ([College Board 2026](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/about-ap-scores/score-distributions)). Algebra-based mechanics is exactly the terrain where AetherLab's pendulum, projectile and orbit components are strongest. **JEE Main** drew about **1.54M registrants** in 2025, roughly ten times AP Physics 1 ([Vedantu](https://www.vedantu.com/jee-main/how-many-students-appeared-for-jee-mains-2025)). **Homeschooling** reached about **3.4M US students**, growing 4.9% a year ([NHERI](https://nheri.org/how-many-homeschool-students-are-there-in-the-united-states/); [JHU](https://education.jhu.edu/edpolicy/policy-research-initiatives/homeschool-hub/homeschool-growth-2024-2025/)), and these families buy curriculum directly and rarely include a physics specialist.

The demand signal pairs high use with low trust. **54% of US teens** use chatbots for schoolwork and 40%+ use them for math problems, yet only about **25% find them very helpful**. 59% believe classmates use AI to cheat ([Pew, Feb 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/)). Among college students, 52% say most or all of their instructors prohibit AI ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/academics/2025/05/20/experts-weigh-everyone-cheating-college)). A tool positioned as "understand and visualize, with sources" faces less institutional resistance than one that looks like an answer engine.

The teacher side may be the stronger wedge. Only **32% of US high-school physics teachers are physics specialists**, 28% have neither a physics degree nor physics-teaching experience, and the shortfall is 15,000–23,000 teachers ([PhysTEC](https://phystec.org/teacher-shortage/)). Out-of-field teachers most need accurate, ready-made visuals. They are also most exposed to the risk Common Sense Media flagged when it rated AI teacher assistants "Moderate Risk": inaccurate output that slips past the teacher ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/ai-teacher-assistants-need-better-safety-measures-common-sense-media-report-finds)). Weekly AI-using teachers report saving **5.9 hours a week** ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx)). Yet only about 15% or fewer of AI-using teachers adopt dedicated lesson-plan tools ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4180-1.html)). That suggests AetherLab should deliver *materials* (MP4, PDF, quiz files) that fit existing workflows rather than asking teachers to adopt another platform. Privacy rules gate K-12 sales: amended COPPA compliance was due April 22, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)). Local-model mode, where student data never leaves the machine, answers that concern directly. With **2.2 billion people still offline** ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/facts-figures-2025/)), offline operation also matters for shared-PC labs, though the local models need capable hardware.

Pricing anchors are low. Wolfram|Alpha Pro costs $2.99/month for students ([Wolfram](https://www.wolframalpha.com/pro/pricing/students)). Khanmigo costs $4/month and is free for teachers ([Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/pricing)). Gauth Plus is about $12/month. Institutional deals land around **$10–30 per user per year**; Cal State paid about $30 per user for ChatGPT Edu ([LAist](https://laist.com/news/education/chatgpt-california-state-university-csu-ai-deal)). Animo's $49 one-time license sets the creator-tool anchor. Distribution through search is broken, since Chegg blamed Google AI Overviews for its 40% subscriber drop ([Chegg Q2 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495425000090/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm)). Better channels are short-form clips of AetherLab's own output, since each lesson is native TikTok/Shorts material; teacher communities, where PhET trained 30,000 teachers through its teacher-leader network in 2025 ([PhET Impact Report](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Impact_Report_2025.pdf)); and a free-for-teachers tier following the Khanmigo pattern.

## Four 2026 technical shifts make a self-contained offline AetherLab feasible

**Manim CE v0.21.0 (August 2026) adds Typst math rendering with no TeX install needed.** It also makes the Cairo renderer about **2.2× faster** and adds parallel partial-movie encoding ([changelog](https://docs.manim.community/en/stable/changelog/0.21.0-changelog.html)). This targets AetherLab's two worst operational problems: fragile Windows LaTeX installs (the source of the `Tex("m_1")` failure class fixed in commit 765a5ea) and render time. Two things need a spike first: the exact Typst mobject API, and coverage of existing LaTeX in the curated components.

For narration, **Kokoro-82M** (Apache 2.0, ONNX, faster than real time on CPU, 8 languages) is small enough to bundle ([HF ONNX](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX); [comparison](https://ocdevel.com/blog/20250720-tts)). **manim-voiceover** provides the bookmark and duration hooks needed to tie highlights to spoken words ([docs](https://voiceover.manim.community/en/stable/index.html)). That is the implementation path for Mayer's largest effect, temporal contiguity. Chatterbox (MIT) enables optional voice cloning. F5-TTS weights are non-commercial and should be avoided ([FindSkill](https://findskill.ai/blog/best-open-source-tts-2026/)). Whisper large-v3-turbo (MIT) covers spoken questions offline ([HF](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo)).

On the LLM side, DashScope supports **JSON Schema structured output** on the Qwen3.7-Plus series ([Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/qwen-structured-output)). That makes schema-valid planner and derivation outputs cheap. Its **explicit context cache bills hits at about 10% of the input price**, with a 5-minute TTL that resets on each hit ([Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/context-cache)), a close fit for a repair loop that resends the same long system prompt. Offline quality is the weak point. ManiBench's 0% for Qwen-2.5-Coder shows small local coders fail without scaffolding. The 94% result for Qwen3-Coder-30B came with RL training and doc-grounded repair ([arXiv 2604.18364](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18364)). That model needs about 19 GB at Q4 ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/tutorials/qwen3-coder-how-to-run-locally)). Offline mode should therefore lean on templates and components, with free-form generation as a fallback.

For the live canvas, **Qt Quick 3D Physics** is now a stable, pure-QML rigid-body API in Qt 6.11. It ships with PySide6-Addons, while PyQt6 bindings were not verified, which could force a PySide6 migration ([Qt docs](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qtquick3dphysics-index.html)). **NVIDIA Warp** (Apache 2.0) offers differentiable Python kernels ([GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA/warp/blob/main/README.md)). Differentiable kernels let a student pose an inverse question such as "what launch angle hits the target" and have it solved by gradient descent. **WebGPU now ships in all major browsers** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers)) and MuJoCo has an official WASM build ([DeepMind](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/main/wasm/README.md)), so a web companion for Chromebook and iPad classrooms is realistic.

**PaddleOCR-VL 1.6** (0.9B, May 2026) converts printed and handwritten formulas to LaTeX at state-of-the-art accuracy ([arXiv 2510.14528](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14528v1)). Together with the Qwen vision model AetherLab already uses, it makes "photograph your homework" input straightforward.

Two things should stay out of the core. Generative video is physically unreliable, and OpenAI's **Sora 2 API is being removed on 24 September 2026** ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). Formal verification in Lean4PHYS/PhysLib has reached college-level physics but remains research-grade ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/2821de2dc3501f55732e59d053e9e0cb-Paper-Conference.pdf)). For packaging, Nuitka generally triggers fewer antivirus false positives than PyInstaller, though the evidence for that is anecdotal ([pythonguis](https://www.pythonguis.com/faq/problems-with-antivirus-software-and-pyinstaller/)).

## A three-stage roadmap: make correctness visible, make lessons active, then widen reach

The priorities follow from three findings. Correctness is the one axis where AetherLab can lead and where errors do the most damage. Engagement design matters more than model quality at scale. Reach is limited by the Windows-only, TeX-dependent install. Each item below is tied to its evidence and to a metric that AetherLab's existing test_features.py-style gates can track.

### Quick wins (0–6 weeks)

| # | Change | Evidence | Metric to gate on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Add a ManimCE-vs-ManimGL **forbidden-construct list** and few-shot examples drawn from `scene_components` to the coder prompt. Before rendering, statically check every called Manim symbol and kwarg against the installed version's signatures. | Forbidden-construct prompting "eliminates version conflicts entirely" ([ManiBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13251)). API hallucination is the #1 crash class ([TheoremExplainAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19400v1)). | Render success, first-try pass rate |
| Q2 | **Deterministic layout check**: bounding-box overlap and off-frame detection on mobjects before render. | Q3 | **Scoped repair** (line → block → scene) and **targeted doc retrieval** for only the functions in the failing code. Bound the critic at 2 text + 2 visual rounds with an auto-pass threshold. | 2.5–5.6× time savings ([Code2Video](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174)); generic RAG hurt GPT-4o, targeted RITL-DOC reached 94% ([arXiv 2604.18364](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18364)); bounded loops ([ManimAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30296)) | Wall-clock per scene, tokens per lesson |
| Q4 | **Physics-aware critic**: pass the verifier's expected facts ("period 2.0 s, apex at t = 1.2 s") to the vision critic and check them against sampled frames. | Caption-and-compare loop more than doubled physical commonsense ([PhyT2V](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00596)) | Fact-check pass rate |
| Q5 | Put a **"verified / unverified" badge** on every numeric claim and derivation step, and a **source chip** on every OpenStax citation. | Wrong videos feel just as convincing ([Kulgemeyer & Wittwer](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8932681/)); teachers miss AI errors ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/ai-teacher-assistants-need-better-safety-measures-common-sense-media-report-finds)) | Share of claims verified |
| Q6 | Storyboard template change: add a **misconception-refutation beat** after the hook (sourced from a curated misconception list, not invented), a **key-symbols pre-training card**, **shorter chapters**, and **no full-sentence burned-in captions**. | Refutation d = 0.79 ([Muller](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2007.00248.x)); pre-training d = 0.46, redundancy d = 0.87 ([Mayer](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jcal.12197)); 80% vs 36% completion willingness for shorter AI videos ([Code2Video](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174)) | Chapter length, quiz scores |

| Q9 | Enable **DashScope explicit context caching** on the planner and coder system prompts. | Hits at about 10% of input price, TTL resets inside repair loops ([Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/context-cache)) | Cost per lesson |

The sequencing matters. The quick wins cost little because the architecture already exists: most are prompt, template or pre-render checks, and they raise measurable pass rates. The medium bets add the verification ledger and the learning loop that turn AetherLab from an animation generator into something a teacher can hand to students. The bigger bets are what make it defensible. B1 and B2 in particular create assets that a prompt-to-Manim clone or a lab's free feature does not have. The list also rules some things out. AetherLab should not invest in a better Socratic chat, which is free elsewhere. It should not add generative video to the core. It should not market "doubles learning" before B5 produces evidence.

## Conclusion

The research changes how the product question should be framed. AetherLab's parts (derivation, animation, narration, quiz, slider) are individually commodities. The durable asset is the **chain of custody for numbers**: one solved, verified set of parameters that flows into the derivation text, the animation's motion, the narration's claims, the critic's checklist, the quiz's answer key and the slider's live model. That chain lets AetherLab *prove* consistency across artifacts. NotebookLM, ChatGPT's widgets and Math-To-Manim generate each artifact independently and cannot do this. The fluency-illusion research makes this more than an engineering nicety. In a medium where wrong content feels exactly as convincing as right content, verification is part of the teaching, not only a quality control step.

The second point is that the field's measurement gap is an opening. There is no benchmark for physically correct educational animation, VLM judges barely track human judgments of accuracy, and no one has tested sliders plus narrated animation in a randomized trial. A small team that publishes honest numbers on its own pipeline would gain an advantage the funded players have not pursued, because their business-video pivot does not need physics to be right. The open uncertainties are real: whether local models can reach cloud-level reliability on typical student hardware, whether Windows desktop distribution can reach K-12 without the web companion, and how large learning effects will be in the field rather than the lab. Each maps to a specific bet above (B4, B2, B5), so they are known risks with a plan attached.
