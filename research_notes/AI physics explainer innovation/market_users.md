# Market, Users, and Business Models for AI Physics/STEM Learning Tools (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-09-23. All figures are from web sources that were searched or fetched. Where a figure comes only from a search-result summary and not from a fetched primary page, it is marked "(snippet)". Market-research-firm sizing varies a great deal between firms and should be treated as indicative only.

## 1. Market size and growth (AI in education, STEM edtech, test prep, tutoring)

### Takeaway
"AI in education" is a small but fast-growing slice, sized anywhere from about $2B to $19B in 2024–25 depending on the firm. It sits inside a much larger tutoring and test-prep spend of roughly $70–150B a year. Venture funding for edtech is at a multi-year low (about $2.6B in 2025) and is going to AI-enabled products with proven traction. The money is in tutoring and test prep. Selling "AI in education" as a category is not where the revenue is.

### Cited Findings
- **Grand View Research:** the AI-in-education market reaches **$32.27B by 2030**, at a **31.2% CAGR** for 2025–2030 — [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/press-release/global-artificial-intelligence-ai-education-market)
- **MarketsandMarkets:** **$2.21B (2024) → $5.82B (2030)**, 17.5% CAGR — [Yahoo Finance / MarketsandMarkets](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-education-market-surges-5-140000060.html)

### Inferences
- The addressable spend for a physics explainer is mostly **tutoring and test-prep budgets**, both families' and schools'. A new "AI in education" budget line exists but is small and poorly defined.
- JEE (about 1.5M a year), AP Physics (about 175k for Physics 1 alone, plus Physics 2 and C), and IB/A-level are concentrated, exam-driven, high-willingness-to-pay pockets. JEE volume is roughly ten times AP Physics 1.
- Venture capital is scarce and goes to traction plus AI. A bootstrapped or low-burn desktop product that has no per-user cloud cost when run on Ollama fits this funding climate.

## 2. Student pain points in physics (hard topics, YouTube/ChatGPT/Chegg use, trust, integrity)

### Takeaway
AP Physics is still one of the hardest AP families. Physics 1 pass rates were about 42–47% until the 2025 exam redesign and are now about 67–68%. Physics 1 still has the highest share of 1s among the physics exams. Most teens (54%) now use chatbots for schoolwork, over 40% for math problems. Cheating is perceived as widespread, and Chegg-style answer lookup has collapsed. That makes room for tools that explain reasoning. Answer dispensers are losing that ground.

### Cited Findings
- **AP Physics 1, May 2025:** 5 = 19.7%, 4 = 24.7%, 3 = 22.9%, 2 = 13.4%, 1 = 19.3%. **Pass rate 67.3%, mean 3.12** — [College Board 2025 PDF](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-physics-1-score-distributions.pdf) (snippet)
- **2024 to 2025 change:** the Physics 1 pass rate went from **about 47% in 2024** to about 66–67% in 2025, after the new hybrid exam format. This was reportedly the biggest year-over-year jump of any AP exam — [Magna Education](https://magnaeducation.ai/2025/07/06/ap-exam-score-distributions/); [GradGPT](https://www.gradgpt.com/ap/blog/is-ap-physics-1-hard) (secondary sources; the exact 2024 figure was not verified against College Board)
- **2026 AP distributions** (fetched from College Board):
  - **Physics 1:** 19/24/25/15/17, **68% pass**
  - **Physics 2:** 20/29/23/21/7, **72%**
  - **Physics C: Mechanics:** 20/25/27/17/11, **72%**
  - **Physics C: E&M:** 24/24/27/17/8, **75%**
  - For comparison: Calculus AB 65%, Calculus BC 82%, Chemistry 75%

  Physics 1 has the highest share of 1s (17%) among the physics exams — [College Board AP Students, 2026 score distributions](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/about-ap-scores/score-distributions)
- **Pew, fall 2025** (1,458 US teens aged 13–17, surveyed Sep 25–Oct 9, 2025, ±3.3 pts):
  - **54%** of teens have used chatbots for schoolwork, and **10%** do all or most of their schoolwork with them
  - **40%+** use chatbots for research or **solving math problems**
  - **59%** think students at their school use AI to cheat at least sometimes. About a third say it happens extremely or very often, and **76% of teens who use chatbots for schoolwork think cheating is common**
  - Only about **25%** find chatbots extremely or very helpful for schoolwork, and about 25% are very confident using them

  — [Pew Research Center, "How Teens Use and View AI" (Feb 24, 2026)](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/)
- **Pew, earlier data:** teen ChatGPT use for schoolwork **doubled from 13% (2023) to 26% (2024)**. **29%** said using it to solve math problems is acceptable and **28%** said it is not; 54% said it is OK for researching topics — [Pew Research Center (Jan 2025)](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/01/15/about-a-quarter-of-us-teens-have-used-chatgpt-for-schoolwork-double-the-share-in-2023/)
- **Tyton Partners, Time for Class 2025** (higher education): **42% of students use genAI weekly or daily**, 10 points more than instructors. Students "prefer human-centered support" and have mixed views on faculty use — [D2L press release](https://www.d2l.com/newsroom/tyton_partners_report_examines_ai_in_higher_education/); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/06/11/3097384/0/en/Tyton-Partners-Releases-2025-Time-for-Class-Report-Institutions-Rebalance-Human-Connection-and-Digital-Innovation-in-Higher-Ed.html). Inside Higher Ed's headline on the same data: "65 Percent of Students Use Gen AI Chat Bot Weekly" — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/academic-life/2025/06/11/65-percent-students-use-gen-ai-chat-bot-weekly). The two figures appear to use different bases, so there is a conflict to note.
- **Tyton Partners, Time for Class 2026** (3,000+ respondents at 750+ US institutions, April–May 2026):
  - Daily genAI use: **32% of students**, 25% of faculty, 43% of administrators
  - Weekly-or-more use now exceeds 50% in all three groups
  - "Paid, out-of-pocket AI use increased across all three groups"

  — [Tyton Partners](https://tytonpartners.com/time-for-class-2026-the-ai-tipping-point-from-monitoring-students-to-engaging-them/)
- **EDUCAUSE Students and Technology Report:**
  - **43%** of students said they don't use AI in coursework
  - **52%** said most or all of their instructors prohibit AI
  - Students' top concerns: being **accused of plagiarism or cheating (33%)** and **over-dependence on AI (30%)**

  — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/academics/2025/05/20/experts-weigh-everyone-cheating-college) (snippet; the EDUCAUSE primary source was not fetched)
- **Chegg's collapse shows the answer-lookup model failing:**
  - Q2 2025: revenue **$105M (−36% YoY)** and **2.6M subscribers (−40% YoY)**, which Chegg attributed largely to **Google AI Overviews** reducing traffic — [Chegg Q2 2025 8-K (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495425000090/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm)
  - Q2 2026: revenue **$51.8M (−51% YoY)**. Chegg says "AI created real headwinds for this company" and is pivoting to an "employability platform". Subscriber count was not disclosed. Chegg Study monthly retention was called "very strong" — [Chegg Q2 2026 8-K (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495426000085/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm)
  - Chegg also cut nearly half its workforce — [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/chegg-layoffs-strategic-alternatives-google-ai/804192/)
  - **Warning:** a search summary claimed "7.5 million active subscribers in Q2 2026". That contradicts the SEC filings, which show 2.6M in Q2 2025 and no count disclosed in Q2 2026. Treat it as unreliable.
- **OpenAI's response to integrity concerns:** it launched **"study mode"** on July 29, 2025. It withholds direct answers in favor of Socratic hints and is available on free and paid plans — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/29/1120801/openai-is-launching-a-version-of-chatgpt-for-college-students/)
- **Physics learning on social media:** a 2026 study in *Research in Science Education* looks at A-level physics students' "rants, resilience and peer pedagogy" on TikTok, showing that students turn to social media for study support and to vent about difficulty — [Springer, Research in Science Education](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11165-026-10332-x)

### Inferences
- **Algebra-based Physics 1** (mechanics: kinematics, forces, energy, momentum, rotation) is the largest and weakest-scoring physics audience. It is a natural beachhead for animated derivations and parameter canvases.
- Students already use general chatbots heavily for physics and math, but only about a quarter find them very helpful. That leaves room for grounded, visual, step-by-step explanations with citations. AetherLab's textbook-grounded citations and physics verifier speak directly to the trust gap.
- Cheating concerns and instructor bans (52% of students report them) mean positioning matters. Framing AetherLab as "understand and visualize" (study-mode-like) rather than "get the answer" lowers institutional friction.
- Chegg's decline shows that answer lookup is commoditized by free AI. Paid value has to come from something free chatbots don't do well: animation, simulation, verified numerics, and polished study artifacts.

### Gaps
- There is no College Board data on which AP Physics units or free-response questions score lowest. Chief Reader reports would have it but were not fetched.
- No surveys were found on physics-specific YouTube use, such as the share of AP students who watch Flipping Physics.
- No quantitative study of LLM error rates on physics problems was gathered in this pass.
- No Reddit or Discord sentiment analysis was performed. Subreddits such as r/APStudents, r/PhysicsStudents and r/JEE were not scraped.

## 3. Teacher/educator pain points (prep time, visuals, labs, assessment, AI policy, privacy, standards)

### Takeaway
Teachers who use AI weekly save about 5.9 hours a week, but 40% of K-12 teachers still don't use it. Institutional AI policy lags badly: only 32% of higher-education administrators report institution-wide policies. Physics in particular has a structural staffing problem: most US physics classes are taught by teachers without a physics degree. Those teachers most need ready-made, accurate visuals and demos. Privacy rules (COPPA amended 2025, compliance by April 22, 2026; FERPA) gate K-12 sales.

### Cited Findings
- **Gallup–Walton Family Foundation** (2,232 US public K-12 teachers, surveyed March 18–April 11, 2025):
  - Weekly AI users save **5.9 hours a week, about six weeks a year**
  - **32%** use AI weekly, 28% infrequently, and **40% not at all**
  - Across nine task types, **60–84%** of users say AI saves time

  — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx); [Walton Family Foundation](https://www.waltonfamilyfoundation.org/six-weeks-giving-teachers-time-back-with-ai)
- **Common teacher AI uses** in the same data: preparing to teach (37%), making worksheets and activities (33%), modifying materials for student needs (28%) — [K-12 Dive / RAND summary](https://www.k12dive.com/news/teacher-ai-training-remains-uneven-despite-uptick/745668/) (snippet)
- **RAND, 2025:** **53%** of ELA, math and **science** teachers used AI for school, up more than 15 points in one to two years. Only about **15% or fewer** of AI-using teachers use tools built specifically for lesson plans. The title finding is that "AI use in schools is quickly increasing but guidance lags behind" — [RAND RRA4180-1](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4180-1.html)
- **Tyton Partners, Time for Class 2025:**
  - **36%** of daily genAI users report a marked decrease in workload
  - Instructors say monitoring student AI use has **created more work**
  - Most institutions lack a genAI policy

  — [D2L](https://www.d2l.com/newsroom/tyton_partners_report_examines_ai_in_higher_education/)
- **Tyton Partners, Time for Class 2026:**
  - Only **32%** of administrators report institution-wide AI policies
  - Faculty rate policy effectiveness at 22%, administrators at 44%
  - "Integration beats restriction": only 13% say restriction works, 30% say integration works
  - Faculty who redesigned assessments (24%) saw a 10+ point attendance advantage

  — [Tyton Partners](https://tytonpartners.com/time-for-class-2026-the-ai-tipping-point-from-monitoring-students-to-engaging-them/)
- **Physics teacher shortage (AIP / PhysTEC):**
  - About **27,000** US high school physics teachers, with a shortfall of **15,000–23,000**
  - Only **32%** are physics "Specialists" (physics degree plus experience); **28%** are "Occasional" physics teachers with neither
  - Only **43% of new physics teachers** have a physics-related degree
  - Most programs prepared **zero** physics teachers over three years

  — [PhysTEC](https://phystec.org/teacher-shortage/); [Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/the-teacher-shortage-in-physics) (snippet; some underlying AIP data may predate 2025)
- **Demand for simulations and visuals (PhET as a benchmark):** in 2025 PhET delivered **250M+ sim runs to 50M+ learners** (1.8B cumulative since 2002). It has **170+ sims in 130+ languages** and launched **"PhET Studio", a premium classroom-customization product** — [PhET Impact Report 2025](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Impact_Report_2025.pdf) (snippet)
- **Accuracy and oversight concerns:** Common Sense Media rated AI teacher assistants **"Moderate Risk"**. It tested Gemini in Google Classroom, Khanmigo Teacher Assistant, Curipod and MagicSchool. Risks include inaccurate information teachers might miss ("invisible influencers") and racial bias in generated behavior plans. It recommends supplementing educators rather than replacing them — [Common Sense Media press release](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/ai-teacher-assistants-need-better-safety-measures-common-sense-media-report-finds); [Risk assessment PDF](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/featured-content/files/csm-ai-risk-assessment-ai-teacher-assistants-final.pdf)
- **COPPA:** the FTC finalized amendments on January 16, 2025. The rule took effect **June 23, 2025**, with **compliance required by April 22, 2026**. The FTC **declined to adopt edtech-specific provisions** and deferred to expected FERPA rule changes from the Department of Education, while continuing to enforce COPPA in edtech under existing guidance — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2025/05/coppa-rule-ftc-amended-childrens-privacy)

### Inferences
- Many physics teachers are out of field. Accurate, pre-verified animations and derivations lower their preparation burden and their risk of teaching errors. That makes them a strong teacher-side wedge, especially because Common Sense flags that inaccurate AI output can slip past teachers.
- Teachers are not adopting dedicated lesson-plan tools (15% or fewer of AI users). They adopt general assistants and ready materials. AetherLab's outputs (video, quiz, study-guide PDF) arrive as materials, which fits how teachers already work.
- **Local-first mode (Ollama) is a privacy selling point.** Student data never leaves the machine, which simplifies COPPA/FERPA/GDPR review. Cloud mode, however, would need a data processing agreement and a student-data-privacy pledge to sell to districts.
- PhET's premium "Studio" move suggests educators will pay for customizable simulations. AetherLab's parameter canvas competes with PhET in the same space.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 quantitative data was found on physics lab constraints (equipment budgets, lab time).
- No data was found on how much teachers value NGSS or AP CED alignment in purchasing decisions. It is widely asserted but unsourced here.
- GDPR and UK/EU school AI guidance was not researched.
- There are no surveys on how long physics teachers spend making visuals or demos specifically.

## 4. Pricing and business models of comparable tools

### Takeaway
Consumer STEM help clusters at **$3–$20 a month**. Big platforms are pushing prices toward zero for students and teachers: Khanmigo is free for teachers, Gemini has been free for students, and ChatGPT study mode is free. Institutional deals land around **$10–$30 per user per year**. A paid physics tool needs a clearly differentiated output (video, animation, verified numerics) or it will lose to free.

### Cited Findings
- **Khanmigo:**
  - **Free for teachers**; **$4/month or $44/year** for learners and parents — [Khanmigo pricing](https://www.khanmigo.ai/pricing)
  - District partnerships reportedly **from $10 per student a year** (enterprise) up to about **$15 per student** — [Khan Academy Blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/becoming-a-khan-academy-districts-partner/); [EduSageAI](https://www.edusageai.com/blogs/how-much-does-khanmigo-cost-pricing-for-teachers-and-schools-in-2026) (secondary)
  - Arizona gave select districts free access through SY2025–26 — [Khan Academy Blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/announcing-free-khanmigo-for-select-arizona-districts/)
- **Brilliant Premium:** about **$20/month billed annually (~$240/year)**, about $30 month-to-month, and about $40/month for a six-seat family plan as of August 2026 — [Nibble / Brighterly summaries](https://brighterly.com/blog/brilliant-org-cost/). Another source lists **$161.88/year** and **$749.99 lifetime** — [My eLearning World](https://myelearningworld.com/brilliant-pricing/). Prices vary with promotions; the official page is [Brilliant Help](https://brilliant.org/help/pricing-and-plans/how-much-does-brilliant-premium-cost/). The sources conflict.
- **Gauth Plus:** **$11.99/month or $99.99/year**. **Photomath Plus:** about **$9.99/month**. **Wolfram|Alpha Pro:** about $7.25/month annual; **student price $2.99/month** — [Wolfram|Alpha Pro student pricing](https://www.wolframalpha.com/pro/pricing/students); [Nibble (Gauth alternatives)](https://nibble-app.com/blog/gauth-alternatives) (snippet). Gauth is a ByteDance product — [review](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/1281/gauth-ai-review-bytedance-homework-app).
- **Google:**
  - US college students were offered **Google AI Pro free for 12 months** (Gemini Pro, Deep Research, NotebookLM Plus, 2TB storage, worth about $240/year) starting April 2025. It later expanded internationally, and new sign-ups reportedly closed by early 2026. After the free period it converts to **$19.99/month** — [TechJack Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/google-gemini/google-gemini-student/); [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/google-one-ai-premium-students-free/) (secondary details; timing is not fully verified)
  - NotebookLM ("Gemini Notebook", reportedly renamed July 2026) is **free** in Google for Education — [Google for Education](https://edu.google.com/intl/ALL_us/ai-gemini-notebook/)
- **ChatGPT Edu:**
  - Cal State paid about **$1.9M for 40,000 users for 6 months**, then **$15M for about 500,000 users** (July 2025–June 2026, **about $30 per user a year**) — [LAist](https://laist.com/news/education/chatgpt-california-state-university-csu-ai-deal)
  - The renewed contract costs **$13M a year for three years** — [EdSource](https://edsource.org/2026/cal-state-renews-controversial-system-wide-contract-with-openai/758919)
  - Study mode is free to all users — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/)
- **Freemium conversion benchmark:** Duolingo paid subscribers are about **9% of MAU** (12.7M out of 140.6M, Q2 2026). That figure is derived from [Duolingo Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm).
- **Chegg's declining subscription model:** Q2 2025 had 2.6M subscribers, but **retention and ARPU rose** even as traffic fell. People who find the service still pay; the failure was in acquisition via Google search — [Chegg Q2 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495425000090/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm)
- **Premium tier on a free simulation base:** PhET launched "PhET Studio" as a premium product for customizing classroom simulations — [PhET Impact Report 2025](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Impact_Report_2025.pdf)
- **Out-of-pocket spending is rising:** paid, out-of-pocket AI use rose among students, faculty and administrators in 2026 — [Tyton Partners 2026](https://tytonpartners.com/time-for-class-2026-the-ai-tipping-point-from-monitoring-students-to-engaging-them/)

### Inferences
- **Price anchors:**
  - $3/month (Wolfram student)
  - $4/month (Khanmigo)
  - $10–12/month (Photomath, Gauth)
  - $20/month (Brilliant, Gemini/ChatGPT Plus)
  - Institutions about $10–30 per user a year
- A local-first desktop app has near-zero marginal cost in Ollama mode. That enables a generous free tier, a one-time-purchase or "pro features" model, or bring-your-own-API-key, none of which cloud competitors can match.
- **B2B2C through teachers:** free for teachers (the Khanmigo pattern), then students or families pay, then a district license. This is the proven route, but district sales require privacy compliance and LMS integration.
- A roughly 9% freemium conversion (Duolingo, best in class) is an upper bound. Typical edtech conversion is likely much lower, but no source was found for that.

### Gaps
- Quizlet Plus pricing and Quizlet's AI features were not verified. Chegg Study's current list price was not found.
- No reliable typical freemium conversion rates for STEM education apps were found.
- ChatGPT Edu list pricing (as opposed to the CSU negotiated rate) is not public.
- Photomath (owned by Google) plan details were not verified on the official page.

## 5. Distribution and growth channels

### Takeaway
Search-driven acquisition is broken: Chegg's traffic fell because of Google AI Overviews. Social video, teacher communities and institutional or LMS deals are now the viable channels. Manim, the 3Blue1Brown engine, has spawned a wave of AI text-to-Manim tools aimed at creators, so the creator-animation segment is real and increasingly contested.

### Cited Findings
- **Search traffic is disappearing for homework-help sites:** Chegg directly blamed Google AI Overviews for lower traffic and a 40% subscriber drop — [Chegg Q2 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495425000090/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm); [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/chegg-layoffs-strategic-alternatives-google-ai/804192/)
- **Manim:** the open-source Python engine made by Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown) is used by educators, researchers and creators. The community edition (ManimCE) was forked in 2020 — [GitHub 3b1b/manim](https://github.com/3b1b/manim); [3Blue1Brown on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3Blue1Brown)
- **AI text-to-Manim tools are proliferating:**
  - **AnimG** (plain English to Manim, rendered, no code) — [AnimG](https://animg.app/en/manim-for-youtube)
  - **Animo** — [Animo](https://animo.video/what-is-manim)

## 6. Underserved segments

### Takeaway
There are four clear underserved pools: offline and low-bandwidth learners (2.2B people offline), the fast-growing US homeschool market (about 3.4M students), Indian exam-prep students (about 1.5M JEE candidates a year), and out-of-field physics teachers. A local-AI desktop app is unusually well suited to the offline and privacy-sensitive segments. Non-English support is table stakes for global reach; PhET runs in 130+ languages.

### Cited Findings
- **Connectivity (ITU):** about **6 billion** people use the internet in 2025, and **2.2 billion remain offline** (the 2024 estimate was revised from 2.6B to 2.3B). Internet use is **93%** in high-income countries and **27%** in low-income countries (2024) — [ITU Facts and Figures 2025](https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/facts-figures-2025/); [ITU internet use](https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/2025/10/15/ff25-internet-use/)
- **Homeschooling:**
  - About **3.4M US homeschool students (K-12) in 2024–25**, about **6.3%** of school-age children, up from 2.5M in spring 2019 — [NHERI](https://nheri.org/how-many-homeschool-students-are-there-in-the-united-states/)
  - Growth averaged **4.9%** in 2024–25, more than double the pre-pandemic rate of about 2%, and **36%** of reporting states hit record highs — [Johns Hopkins Institute for Education Policy](https://education.jhu.edu/edpolicy/policy-research-initiatives/homeschool-hub/homeschool-growth-2024-2025/)
- **India exam prep:** JEE Main 2025 had **1.54M registrants** — [Vedantu](https://www.vedantu.com/jee-main/how-many-students-appeared-for-jee-mains-2025)
- **Localization demand:** PhET's 170+ sims in **130+ languages** reached 50M+ learners in 2025 — [PhET Impact Report 2025](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Impact_Report_2025.pdf)
- **Out-of-field physics teachers:** 28% of US high school physics teachers have neither a physics degree nor experience teaching it — [PhysTEC](https://phystec.org/teacher-shortage/)
- **Unequal access at university level:** a large 2026 undergraduate AI-use study reports "disparities in access — and in cheating" — [Berkeley News (May 21, 2026)](https://news.berkeley.edu/2026/05/21/the-largest-study-of-ai-use-by-undergrads-is-in-revealing-disparities-in-access-and-in-cheating/) (headline only; not fetched)

### Inferences
- **Offline and low-bandwidth:** a local Ollama mode with bundled content is a real differentiator against every cloud-only competitor (Khanmigo, Gemini, ChatGPT, Brilliant). Hardware is the limit, since local LLMs need a capable PC. The realistic offline audience is schools and labs with shared PCs, or middle-income students, more than the lowest-income regions.
- **Homeschool families** buy curriculum directly, lack a physics specialist, and value visual lessons, quizzes and study guides. That matches AetherLab's full output bundle.
- **University lecturers and content creators** need explainer animations, and the text-to-Manim entrants confirm the demand. They will pay for quality and correctness, which suits a "pro" tier.

### Gaps
- No data was found on local-LLM-capable PC penetration among students in developing countries.
- No data was found on non-English physics content supply gaps (for example Hindi, Spanish, Arabic, Kazakh or Russian physics video).
- No quantitative data was found on university lecturers' spending on animation or visualization tools.

## 7. Signals of demand (surveys, trends, communities)

### Takeaway
Survey signals show AI study use rising fast and crossing into majority weekly use in higher education by 2026. Pew finds 54% of teens using chatbots for schoolwork. Students spend their own money more, but trust and satisfaction are modest: only about 25% of teens find chatbots very helpful. That gap is the opening for a verified, visual physics tool.

### Cited Findings
- **Teen trajectory (Pew):** teen ChatGPT schoolwork use was **13% (2023) → 26% (2024)**, and teen chatbot schoolwork use reached **54% (fall 2025)** — [Pew Jan 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/01/15/about-a-quarter-of-us-teens-have-used-chatgpt-for-schoolwork-double-the-share-in-2023/); [Pew Feb 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/). The two questions differ (ChatGPT versus any chatbot), so the comparison is directional only.
- **Higher education (Tyton):** weekly-plus genAI use exceeded 50% for students, faculty and administrators for the first time in 2026, and paid out-of-pocket use grew — [Tyton 2026](https://tytonpartners.com/time-for-class-2026-the-ai-tipping-point-from-monitoring-students-to-engaging-them/)
- **K-12 teachers:** 60% used AI in SY2024–25, 32% weekly — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx). RAND puts use at 53% among ELA, math and science teachers in 2025 — [RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4180-1.html)
- **Simulation demand is large and steady:** 250M+ PhET sim runs in 2025 — [PhET Impact Report 2025](https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Impact_Report_2025.pdf)
- **Big-tech investment in student AI is a demand signal and a competitive threat:**
  - OpenAI study mode (July 2025) — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/)
  - Google's free AI Pro for students — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/google-one-ai-premium-students-free/)
  - CSU's $13M-a-year ChatGPT Edu renewal — [EdSource](https://edsource.org/2026/cal-state-renews-controversial-system-wide-contract-with-openai/758919)
- **Creator demand:** multiple text-to-Manim startups and agent skills appeared in 2025–26 — [AnimG](https://animg.app/en/manim-for-youtube); [GitHub manim-skills](https://github.com/iart-ai/manim-skills)

### Inferences
- The demand for AI study help is proven. The unmet need is **trustworthy, visual and physics-specific** help: satisfaction is low, cheating fears are high, and teachers worry about accuracy.
- Positioning AetherLab as "verified physics you can see", with citations, numeric checks and simulations, targets the gap between high usage and low trust.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or Discord thread analysis, Google Trends pulls, or App Store and Play review mining was done in this pass. These would give qualitative user language and should be a follow-up.
- A Quizlet "How America Learns" report and a Chegg Global Student Survey for 2025 were not located or verified.
