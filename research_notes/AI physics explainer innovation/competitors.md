# Competitive & Adjacent-Product Landscape for AI Physics/STEM Explanation, Animation & Simulation (as of Sept 2026)

Context: positioning AetherLab, a Windows desktop (PyQt6) AI physics workbench with an LLM derivation, OpenStax RAG citations, AI-storyboarded Manim animation (self-healing render plus a vision critic), narrated multi-chapter lessons (transcript, fact-check, quiz, study-guide PDF), a live parametric slider canvas, and local AI (Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint).

Research notes: about 33 tool calls. news.ycombinator.com could not be fetched (WebFetch was blocked), so HN comment sentiment could not be read directly. Reddit threads did not show up in search results. Several "2026 review" sites that came up are SEO or affiliate blogs, and they are flagged where used.

## Q1. LLM-to-Manim / AI math-animation generators (open-source vs paid; output quality)

### Takeaway
This space is crowded and moving fast. It splits into (a) open-source repos/agents (Generative Manim, Math-To-Manim, many small GitHub clones and academic agents) and (b) paid wrappers (Animo desktop at $49 one-time, AnimG browser freemium, and YC-backed general "explainer video" startups Knowlify and Lamina Labs/Simi, which have moved away from Manim toward whiteboard/business video). None of the ones found combine textbook-grounded citations, a numeric/physics verifier, and an interactive simulation canvas. Published benchmarks show the typical failure modes are Manim API hallucination, LaTeX errors, and poor spatial layout.

### Cited Findings
**Generative Manim / Animo (Marcelo Arias, "360macky")**
- Open-source suite (Apache 2.0) that generates Manim code from natural language. About 918 GitHub stars, 133 forks, 410 commits. Supports 20+ models (OpenAI GPT-4o/o1-mini/GPT-5.x, Claude Sonnet/Opus, Gemini 2.5/3 Flash, open models via Featherless). The project is also training open models (Qwen/DeepSeek/CodeLlama bases) with an SFT→DPO→GRPO pipeline that uses Manim's deterministic rendering as the reward signal. — [GitHub: marcelo-earth/generative-manim](https://github.com/marcelo-earth/generative-manim)
- The commercial sibling **Animo** is a desktop app (Windows/Mac/Linux, "under 7MB") with local rendering, real-time preview, MP4/GIF/PNG export in 16:9, 9:16 and 1:1, voiceover via the manim-voiceover plugin, interactive 3D viewing of scene objects, and templates covering physics, algebra, astronomy and chemistry. It works through AI coding CLIs (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Gemini CLI). — [animo.video](https://animo.video/)
- Animo pricing: **$49 one-time** (marked down from $149), lifetime license, no per-video cost. Users bring their own ChatGPT/Claude subscription; otherwise AI credits cost $20/mo after free credits. Claims "1,000+ creators" including users at Columbia, Berkeley, Harvard, Yale, Stanford and Oxford (self-reported). — [animo.video](https://animo.video/); [Animo pricing](https://animo.video/pricing)
- Animo publishes its own "7 best AI Manim tools" list: Animo, AnimG, ManimAI, GateKeep AI (AI STEM tutoring with animated explanations), MotionShark, Manim Video Generator (open source), and Manimate. This is a competitor-authored list and is biased. — [animo.video/ai-manim-tools](https://animo.video/ai-manim-tools)

**Math-To-Manim (HarleyCoops / Christian H. Cooper)**
- MIT-licensed. **About 2.6k stars, 286 forks, 435 commits** per the GitHub page fetched Sept 2026. A secondary source reported 2.4k stars / 255 forks earlier. The repo was created Jan 20, 2025. — [GitHub: HarleyCoops/Math-To-Manim](https://github.com/HarleyCoops/Math-To-Manim); [ailinklab summary](https://www.ailinklab.com/en/opensource/math-to-manim/)
- Pipeline: reverse prerequisite mapping, then forward curriculum, then math validation, then camera/composition planning, then Manim code, then validation and render. It has four independent backends: "Mythos" (Claude CLI, a six-agent chain), "Sol" (Codex CLI with durable, resumable specialist stages), Grok 4.6 (with an offline mode), and GLM (Z.ai). Interfaces are CLI, MCP server and REST API. Each run is saved in a timestamped directory with reasoning artifacts and validation reports. It covers math and physics questions and also produces "study notes". — [GitHub](https://github.com/HarleyCoops/Math-To-Manim)

**Browser SaaS**
- **AnimG**: browser-based Manim AI generator and online editor. Free plan allows 2 animations with a watermark and 3 playground renders a day at 480p. Pro gives unlimited 1080p watermark-free renders plus AI-assisted code fixes. The Pro price was not shown on the fetched page. — [animg.app](https://animg.app/en); [AnimG subscription](https://animg.app/en/subscription)
- **TMA.live**: Show HN (Jan 2025), "AI that generates 3blue1brown-style explainer videos" from any prompt using Manim. The thread content could not be fetched. — [HN item 42590290](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42590290)
- Other open-source examples: rohitg00/manim-video-generator (GPT to Manim web app), Dennis-Diehl/math-video-agent, Open-Models-AI/manim-agent, Hmzbo/manim-animator (an "agent skill"), ManojINaik/manimAnimationAgent. — [manim-video-generator](https://github.com/rohitg00/manim-video-generator); [math-video-agent](https://github.com/Dennis-Diehl/math-video-agent); [manim-animator](https://github.com/Hmzbo/manim-animator); [GitHub topic: manim](https://github.com/topics/manim)

**YC-backed "text to explainer video" startups**
- **Knowlify (YC S25)**: "ElevenLabs for explainer videos". You upload a document or type a prompt, and it writes the script, builds a scene storyboard, adds voiceover and animation. It claims **223K+ videos generated** and NYC Times Square billboards by the end of 2025. It is now positioned mainly for business (training, onboarding, product explainers), though it started from a frustration with textbooks. — [YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OAm-knowlify-the-elevenlabs-for-explainer-videos); [knowlify.com](https://www.knowlify.com/); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/knowlify)
- **Lamina Labs / Simi (YC P26, founded 2025, reportedly raised $4.8M)**: turns a prompt or document into a *whiteboard-style* explainer video "in seconds". Videos run from 1 minute to 1 hour, in 80+ languages, via web app or API, for training, sales, education and YouTube creators. The CTO (Kartikesh Mishra) has said Manim was built before the LLM era and is hard to fine-tune for, so they built their own "near-real-time structured video" infrastructure. Pricing is not disclosed. The $4.8M figure comes from an aggregator. — [YC launch: Simi](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/REC-simi-the-fastest-ai-whiteboard-explainer-video-generator); [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/lamina-labs); [Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/nepali-duo-goes-from-kathmandu-valley-to-silicon-valley); [mer.vin review](https://mer.vin/2026/05/simi-by-lamina-labs-whiteboard-explainer-videos-from-prompts-and-documents/)

**Output quality: benchmarks and tests**
- **TheoremExplainAgent (ACL 2025)**: an agent that makes long (over 5 min) Manim theorem videos, plus TheoremExplainBench (240 theorems across STEM, 5 automated metrics). The o3-mini agent reached a **93.8% success rate** with an overall score of 0.77. The main errors were nonexistent Manim functions/properties/assets, wrong signatures, and LaTeX rendering errors. Most videos had minor layout problems. The paper also notes that multimodal (video) explanations expose reasoning flaws that text hides. — [arXiv 2502.19400](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19400v2); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.332/)
- Follow-up work (e.g., **Code2Video**, Oct 2025) focuses on the layout problem and reports element-layout scores improving from 0.59 to 0.91 in later work. That number comes from a search summary and should be verified against the paper. — [Code2Video arXiv 2510.01174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01174)
- **Manimator** (arXiv 2507.14306) turns research papers and math concepts into Manim animations. Other work describes human-in-the-loop pipelines because "generated code or explanation is not fully correct" without expert review. — [Manimator arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14306v1)
- Blog test (Feb 11, 2026) of Gemini 3 Flash, GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Kimi 2.5 writing Manim. Gemini was best, "the only model that seemed to have any intuition about how objects should be arranged in 3D space". Failures included Claude producing blank screens, Kimi drawing an 8x9 "chessboard", GPT violating timing constraints, hallucinated properties, and renders that took close to an hour. — [jdhwilkins.com](https://www.jdhwilkins.com/vibemanim-ing-spatial-reasoning-and-geminis-secret-superpower/)

### Inferences
- Open-source code generation is commoditized: several free repos and Claude Code/Codex "skills" already do prompt-to-Manim. The differentiation is in **reliability and correctness layers**: self-healing render, a vision critic, a numeric ground truth, and physics verification. Math-To-Manim is the closest open-source analogue (multi-agent, math validation, MCP) and is AetherLab's most direct conceptual competitor. Animo is the closest *product* analogue (desktop, local render, one-time price, physics templates).
- Well-funded startups (Knowlify, Lamina) are moving toward generic business explainer video rather than rigorous physics. That leaves "physically correct, cited, interactive physics" relatively open.
- None of the tools found pair their animation with a **live parametric simulation** or **textbook citations**. Animo's "interactive 3D viewing" is the only interactivity mentioned.
- The benchmark failure modes (API hallucination, LaTeX, layout) are exactly the ones AetherLab's self-healing render, text-mode math repair and vision critic target. That is a defensible technical story if it can be quantified, for example by reporting TheoremExplainBench-style metrics.

### Gaps
- Animo, AnimG and Knowlify user and revenue numbers beyond self-reported claims were not found. AnimG Pro and Simi prices were not found.
- Hacker News comments on TMA.live and other Show HNs could not be fetched (domain blocked). Whether TMA.live is still active is unknown.
- No independent side-by-side quality comparison of Animo, AnimG, Math-To-Manim and Knowlify output was found.
- 3Blue1Brown has made no official statement or product on AI-generated Manim (none found).

## Q2. Interactive simulation incumbents (PhET, Brilliant, GeoGebra, Desmos, Algodoo, oPhysics, Physics Classroom, Wolfram)

### Takeaway
The incumbents have enormous reach (PhET about 250M sim runs a year) but mostly **fixed, hand-built** simulations. AI is being bolted on at the edges: Brilliant's Koji tutor, Wolfram's Notebook Assistant, and third-party PhET AI overlays. OpenAI now ships free built-in interactive STEM visuals in ChatGPT, which directly overlaps with AetherLab's slider canvas, but only for about 70 pre-selected concepts.

### Cited Findings
- **PhET (CU Boulder)**: free HTML5 simulations for physics, chemistry, biology, earth science and math. **250M+ simulation runs a year (~50% in the USA), about 1.9B cumulative uses since 2002** (2024 data). This figure comes from secondary sources and aggregators; historical figures are 15M runs in 2010 and 100M cumulative by 2013. — [phet.colorado.edu](https://phet.colorado.edu/); [EdTech Digest 2024](https://www.edtechdigest.com/2024/09/06/phet-interactive-simulations/); [HundrED](https://hundred.org/en/innovations/phet-interactive-simulations); [ResearchGate 100M case study (older)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265593933_Over_One_Hundred_Million_Simulations_Delivered_A_Case_Study_of_the_PhET_Interactive_Simulations)
- No official PhET generative-AI feature was found. Third parties are adding AI on top: the **Pengi** Chrome extension reads live PhET sim state and asks the learner guiding questions, and **SPYRAL** markets itself as an "AI-powered PhET alternative" with an AI tutor that explains after each interaction. Both are vendor blogs. — [Pengi blog](https://pengi.ai/blog/phet-simulations-ai-tutor-real-time-guidance-inside-the-lab); [SPYRAL blog](https://tryspyral.com/blog/phet-simulations-alternative-2026)
- **Brilliant**: Premium costs $27.99/mo or $161.88/yr (about $13.49/mo), with a $749.99 lifetime option and a 7-day trial. The free tier unlocks only the first chapter of each course. **Koji** is its AI tutor inside every course (unlimited on Premium). It has 100+ STEM courses including physics. No subscriber count was found. — [Brilliant help: pricing](https://brilliant.org/help/pricing-and-plans/how-much-does-brilliant-premium-cost/); [Brilliant free vs Premium](https://brilliant.org/help/pricing-and-plans/what-s-the-difference-between-free-and-premium/); [e-student review 2026](https://e-student.org/brilliant-org-review/)
- **Wolfram**: the Notebook Assistant plus LLM Kit (introduced Dec 2024) uses LLMs with RAG and "computation-augmented generation" (CAG) grounded in Wolfram Language. Subscription tiers are Essentials and Pro, with Wolfram Service Credits for heavy use. Exact prices were not found in results. — [Wolfram Notebook Assistant](https://www.wolfram.com/notebook-assistant-llm-kit/); [Stephen Wolfram, Dec 2024](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/12/useful-to-the-point-of-being-revolutionary-introducing-wolfram-notebook-assistant/); [Press release](https://company.wolfram.com/press-center/wolfram-notebook-assistant-llm-kit/)
- **ChatGPT interactive visuals (OpenAI, launched Mar 10, 2026)**: interactive visualizations for **70+ math and science concepts** where users adjust variables and watch graphs and formulas update live. Examples include Ohm's law, exponential decay, binomial squares, compound interest and trig identities. Available **free to all logged-in users worldwide**, with more modules promised. The article frames these as a fixed set of launch modules, not arbitrary generated simulations. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-now-explains-math-and-physics-with-interactive-visualizations/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/chatgpt-will-now-generate-interactive-visuals-to-help-you-with-math-and-science-concepts-170000520.html)
- **Gemini** has added "interactive images/diagrams": static diagrams become explorable, and learners tap labeled parts for explanations (LearnLM-based). — [gend.co](https://www.gend.co/blog/gemini-interactive-images); [Google blog: Guided Learning](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/)
- **GeoGebra / Desmos**: no branded GeoGebra AI tool was found as of 2026. Desmos is free and needs no account for basic use. Both sources are low-authority listicles. — [JustLearn GeoGebra alternatives](https://justlearn.com/alternatives/geogebra/); [EduGenius](https://www.edugenius.app/blog/best-free-ai-tools-for-math-in-2026-2027)

### Inferences
- PhET's scale shows that teachers trust curated, research-validated simulations. PhET's weakness is that each sim is hand-built, so a problem outside its roughly 150-sim catalog has no simulation. AetherLab's "any problem → parametric canvas" addresses this, but it has to earn PhET-level trust through its verification and citations.
- ChatGPT's free interactive visuals make "slider + formula" a table-stakes feature for mainstream topics. AetherLab should compete on the **long tail**: arbitrary problems, multi-body/3D, derivation-linked sliders, offline use and citations. It should not compete on basic Ohm's-law widgets.
- Wolfram is the correctness benchmark ("computation-augmented generation"). AetherLab's numeric ground truth plus physics verifier is a similar idea at a student price point.

### Gaps
- Algodoo, oPhysics and Physics Classroom: no 2025–2026 AI announcements or usage data were found. Algodoo appears effectively dormant (unverified). These tools were not researched in depth within the call budget.
- No specific GeoGebra or Desmos AI feature announcements were confirmed. The Desmos/Amplify and GeoGebra/BYJU's ownership context was not researched.
- Wolfram|Alpha consumer Pro pricing and Notebook Assistant tier prices were not found.
- Brilliant subscriber counts were not found.

## Q3. AI tutors and homework helpers (Khanmigo, Photomath, Socratic, Gauth, Brainly, Chegg, ChatGPT Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning/LearnLM, Claude learning mode, NotebookLM, Microsoft Copilot)

### Takeaway
The general-purpose LLM vendors (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Microsoft) now give away Socratic "learning modes" for free or bundle them for institutions, and Google's NotebookLM produces animated, source-grounded video explainers. This has collapsed paid homework help (Chegg revenue -39% in 2025, 81% headcount decline from peak). The remaining weaknesses are physics correctness, generic rather than physics-specific visuals, cloud-only operation, and limited teacher control outside the Microsoft and Khan ecosystems.

### Cited Findings

- **Gemini Guided Learning** is free for all Gemini users, Socratic and step-by-step, and built on **LearnLM** (Google's pedagogy-tuned model family). In a Sierra Leone study (1,763 junior secondary students, 8 weeks, at least 12 hours of use), students gained the equivalent of moving from the 50th to the 64th percentile in math. — [Google blog: Guided Learning](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/); [Google for Education: Gemini](https://edu.google.com/intl/ALL_us/ai/gemini-for-education/); [LearnLM on Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/solutions/learnlm); [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/how-to/geminis-guided-learning-mode-from-google-ai-what-educators-need-to-know)
- **Claude for Education**: Learning Mode is Socratic, and students at partner universities get free Pro-equivalent access. Partners include Northeastern, Syracuse, Champlain, Dartmouth, UVA, Pitt, USF, Columbia, Stanford, LSE and Northumbria (third-party compiled list). Outcome claims in the search summary are unsourced marketing and are not repeated here. — [Anthropic announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education?); [Stanford UIT](https://uit.stanford.edu/service/claude); [Northeastern guide](https://learning.northeastern.edu/ai-student-guides-using-claude-learning-mode-to-study/); [beginnersinai](https://beginnersinai.org/claude-for-education-program/)

**Physics correctness evidence (research)**
- Studies found that GPT-4 scored about 50% on physics exams, graders noted "plausible-sounding" wrong answers, and students (even advanced ones) over-trusted and copy-pasted ChatGPT output without spotting errors. These are mostly 2023 papers and are older. — [arXiv 2309.05163](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.05163v1); [arXiv 2309.03087](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.03087); [PRPER Jun 2023 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/51fc1c9c9441486ca9a5b6ce09295bd0)

### Inferences
- "Socratic tutor chat" is now free from every major lab, so AetherLab cannot differentiate on tutoring dialogue. It should differentiate on **artifacts**: verified derivation, cited textbook sections, a physics-correct animation, an interactive sim, a quiz and a study-guide PDF, produced in one run.
- NotebookLM Cinematic is the most direct threat to "narrated video lessons". However, it is paywalled (Ultra at launch), its generative-video visuals are not physically exact, and it depends on the user's own uploads. AetherLab's Manim visuals are deterministic, equation-exact, and include OpenStax citations by default, which is a real contrast.
- Chegg's collapse shows students will not pay for answers alone. Willingness to pay is shifting to tools that produce understanding and study artifacts, and to teacher and institution tools.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 data was found on Brainly, Socratic (Google has largely folded it into Lens/Gemini; unverified), or Photomath MAU.
- No quantitative 2025–2026 physics-accuracy benchmark for current frontier models in tutoring mode was found. The research cited is 2023.
- Actual usage numbers for ChatGPT Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning and NotebookLM Video Overviews were not found. One aggregator's "140 million ChatGPT users" learning claim is not verified.

## Q4. AI video lesson generators and reviews/user sentiment; exploitable gaps

### Takeaway
Avatar-video tools (Synthesia) target corporate L&D, not physics. Generative-video "explainer" tools (NotebookLM Cinematic, Knowlify, Simi) optimize for speed and polish over technical exactness. Across reviews and research, the recurring complaints are **hallucinated or incorrect content, visual inaccuracies and layout glitches, lack of verification, and cloud/paywall dependence**. None of the products found offers offline/local-model operation plus textbook grounding plus a physics verifier plus interactive simulation plus teacher-exportable lesson packs.

### Cited Findings
- **Synthesia**: free plan; Starter about $18–29/mo (125+ avatars, 10 video min/mo); Creator about $64–89/mo; Enterprise custom. Aimed at training and communications content. One pricing blog cites "leaked roadmap documents" for a $149/mo "Educator" plan in Q3 2026. ### Inferences (gaps AetherLab could exploit)
1. **Correctness you can see.** Competitors either generate unverified Manim code or generative video with "visual inaccuracies". An explicit numeric ground truth, a physics verifier and a vision critic, surfaced to the user as a "verified" badge or fact-check report, speak directly to the most documented weakness.
2. **Textbook grounding by default.** NotebookLM grounds only in user-uploaded sources, and ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude do not cite a curriculum. OpenStax citations with section references are unusual and map to courses teachers already use.
3. **Open-ended interactivity.** ChatGPT covers about 70 fixed concepts and PhET about 150 hand-built sims. Neither generates a manipulable simulation for an arbitrary problem, which is AetherLab's slider canvas.
4. **Offline, privacy and cost.** Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint support fits schools with data-privacy constraints, poor connectivity, or no budget for Ultra/Pro subscriptions. None of the major players offers this.
5. **One run, full lesson pack.** Derivation, animation, narrated chapters, transcript, quiz and study-guide PDF are exportable for teachers. Microsoft Learning Zone and Khanmigo offer teacher tooling but not physics-exact animation.
6. **Price anchor.** Animo at $49 one-time and Khanmigo at $4/mo set low expectations. Brilliant ($13–28/mo) and NotebookLM Ultra sit at the high end.
- Risks: Windows-only desktop limits the Chromebook-heavy K-12 market. Frontier labs ship free STEM features quickly (ChatGPT visuals, Gemini interactive diagrams). Math-To-Manim is free, MIT-licensed and improving.
